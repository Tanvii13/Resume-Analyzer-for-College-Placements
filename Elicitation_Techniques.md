# Resume Analyser - Elicitation Techniques

This document describes how requirements will be collected from the different stakeholders and end users of the Resume Analyser system.

For each stakeholder, the suitable elicitation techniques are listed along with the reason for using them and the information expected from the session.

---

## 1. End Users

End users are the people who directly use the system or use the information generated by it.

| **Stakeholder**                                          | **Recommended Technique(s)**                                                            | **Why This Technique Is Suitable**                                                                                                                                                                                                                        | **What We Need to Find Out**                                                                                                                                                   |
| -------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Students / Job Seekers**                               | Surveys / Questionnaires; Semi-Structured Interviews; Observation; Prototype Evaluation | Surveys can collect feedback from many students, while interviews provide more detailed information. Observation and prototype evaluation help us understand how students actually use the resume analysis and feedback.                                  | Resume formats used, important resume sections, expected feedback, preferred level of explanation, job/internship matching needs, accessibility needs.                         |
| **Placement Cell / Placement Officers**                  | Semi-Structured Interviews; Document Analysis; Requirements Workshop                    | Interviews help understand the current placement process. Existing reports and spreadsheets show what information is already collected. A workshop can be used to discuss requirements with other important stakeholders.                                 | Placement process, placement reports, required student information, dashboards, access permissions, placement-readiness criteria.                                              |
| **Faculty / Placement Coordinators / Career Counselors** | Interviews; Focus Groups; Observation; Prototype Evaluation                             | Interviews and focus groups help collect their views about student guidance. Observation helps understand how counselling is currently done. A prototype can be used to check whether the system's feedback is useful during counselling.                 | Type of feedback required, explanation of recommendations, student progress tracking, ability to add comments, preferred language and format.                                  |
| **Recruiters / HR Professionals**                        | Structured / Semi-Structured Interviews; Observation; Document Analysis                 | Interviews can identify important resume and candidate requirements. Observation can show how resumes are screened. Job descriptions and screening criteria provide additional information about required skills.                                         | Required resume information, important skills, must-have and optional skills, matching expectations, acceptable resume formats, explanation of matching results.               |
| **Hiring Managers**                                      | Scenario-Based Interviews; Document Analysis; Prototype / Demo Review                   | Realistic hiring scenarios can help understand what information a manager needs before making a decision. Existing hiring criteria can show the important selection factors. A prototype can be reviewed to check whether the results are understandable. | Candidate information required for review, important selection criteria, trust in recommendations, information needed to verify a recommendation, human decision requirements. |

| **Stakeholder**              | **Recommended Technique(s)**                              | **Why This Technique Is Suitable**                                                                                                                                                                                             | **What We Need to Find Out**                                                                                                              |
| ---------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Data Privacy Reviewer**    | Document Analysis; Structured Interview                   | Privacy requirements can be identified from applicable rules, college policies, and discussions with the person responsible for data handling.                                                                                 | Consent requirements, data collection limits, storage duration, access permissions, deletion requirements, privacy responsibilities.      |
| **Bias / Fairness Reviewer** | Interviews; Scenario-Based Elicitation; Document Analysis | Interviews can identify concerns about unfair recommendations. Scenarios can be used to discuss how the system should behave in potentially unfair cases. ## 6. Recommended Elicitation Sequence

The elicitation activities should be performed in a practical order so that the requirements become clearer as the project progresses.

### Step 1: Study Existing Information

First, review the available documents such as:

* Placement reports
* Existing forms and spreadsheets
* Project guidelines
* Job descriptions
* Relevant privacy policies
* Available job portal documentation

This gives the team an initial understanding of the existing process and requirements.

### Step 2: Conduct Interviews and Surveys

Next, collect information directly from stakeholders.

* Surveys can be used for a large number of students.
* Interviews can be used with placement officers, faculty, recruiters, managers, and technical stakeholders.
* Questions should cover functional, non-functional, and domain requirements.

### Step 3: Observe Existing Activities

Where possible, observe activities such as:

* Students preparing or reviewing resumes
* Placement counselling
* Resume screening
* Other relevant placement activities

Observation helps identify requirements that stakeholders may not mention during interviews.

### Step 4: Conduct Group Discussions / Workshops

Requirements workshops and focus groups can be used to discuss requirements with multiple stakeholders together.

This helps identify:

* Conflicting requirements
* Common requirements
* Important system features
* Requirements that need agreement between stakeholders

### Step 5: Prepare and Evaluate a Prototype

A simple prototype of the Resume Analyser can then be shown to students, faculty, recruiters, or other relevant stakeholders.

The prototype can help collect feedback about:

* Resume upload
* Resume analysis results
* Skill extraction
* Resume score
* Job matching
* Recommendations
* User interface

### Step 6: Record and Validate Requirements

Finally, all collected requirements should be documented and checked with the relevant stakeholders.

Each requirement should be connected to:

* The stakeholder who provided it
* The elicitation technique used
* The expected system behaviour
* The method that will be used to verify the requirement

## 8. Overview

Different stakeholders require different elicitation techniques because they have different roles and different types of information.

* **Students** can be reached through surveys, interviews, observation, and prototype evaluation to understand their resume-analysis and job-matching needs.
* **Placement officers and faculty / counselors** can provide information about placement procedures, student guidance, reports, and counselling requirements.
* **Recruiters and hiring managers** can provide information about resume screening, required skills, candidate information, and job matching.
* **The development team, QA team, UI/UX designer, system administrator, and project manager** provide technical, usability, testing, security, and project requirements.
* **The academic supervisor** provides requirements related to the project objectives and evaluation criteria.
* **Privacy and fairness stakeholders** help identify requirements related to responsible handling of resume data and fair system behaviour.
* **Job portals and external organizations** are mainly studied through available documents, job descriptions, interfaces, surveys, and information provided through accessible representatives.
* **College management and rejected candidates** can provide additional requirements about reporting, transparency, feedback, and human review.

The collected information will be used to identify and document the **functional requirements, non-functional requirements, and domain requirements** of the Resume Analyser system.
